Not every agency uses everything. Two screens decide what this workspace does and what it talks
to, and what you switch off disappears from the menu, from the client record and from Settings
without a single row of data going anywhere.

![The settings index, grouped by what each group governs.](/img/docs/en-settings.png)

## Two screens, and the difference between them

- **Instellingen → Modules** (`/settings/modules`) — what schakl. itself does for this
  workspace: clients, tasks, projects, hours, invoicing, domains, reporting. Worth having with
  every third-party account in the world cancelled.
- **Instellingen → Integraties** (`/settings/integrations`) — which outside services schakl.
  talks to: Google, Cloudflare, OXXA, Mollie, SnelStart, WordPress, Uptime Kuma, Tag Manager,
  Google Ads. Each holds a credential of yours, and what it stores is a mirror of state that
  lives over there.

The test is one sentence: *if the vendor went out of business tomorrow, is the thing gone, or is
it merely poorer?* Gone means integration. Poorer means module. Each screen sits first in the
group of settings it governs, so a heading and the screen under it cannot disagree.

Both are there for anyone holding the "Huisstijl en modules wijzigen" (Change branding and
enabled modules) permission; by default that is the owner and the administrator.

Missing a module you expected? Then it is not part of this installation.
*Instellingen → Systeeminformatie* (Settings → System information) lists what the installation
itself carries under **Ingeschakelde modules** (Enabled modules).

## What the screen shows

Three kinds of row:

- **Klanten** (Clients) carries the label **Altijd aan** (Always on) and cannot be unticked.
  Almost everything in schakl. hangs off a client, so that module is the hub and stays.
- A licensed module with no valid key installed carries the label **Licentie vereist** (License
  required) and cannot be ticked. The note beside it reads: "Deze module vereist een
  licentiesleutel. Installeer er een onder Instellingen → Licentie en schakel de module daarna
  hier in." (This module requires a license key. Install one under Settings → License, then
  enable it here.)
- Every other module is a plain tick box.

Saving applies to the whole list at once: what is ticked goes on, what is not ticked goes off.

## What switching a module off actually does

The screen says it itself: a disabled module disappears from view everywhere but keeps its
data, and switching it back on restores everything. Concretely:

- its item disappears from the sidebar;
- its panel disappears from the client record;
- its settings screen disappears from *Instellingen*;
- its dashboard tiles can no longer be chosen under *Instellingen → Dashboard*;
- its e-mail templates are no longer listed under *Instellingen → E-mail*;
- a report section it contributes no longer appears in new reports. Reports already generated
  keep their own snapshot and do not change.

Nothing is deleted. Switch the module back on later and everything is there exactly as you left
it.

<Aside type="caution" title="Switching off is not an access control">
Modules decide what is on screen for everybody, not who may do what. If one employee should not
see the hours or the invoices, that belongs under *Instellingen → Rollen* (Settings → Roles).
</Aside>

## What needs what, and who works it out

An integration that has nowhere to put its data without a module says so, and the screen acts on
it rather than refusing you afterwards.

| Integration | Cannot work without |
| --- | --- |
| Cloudflare, OXXA | Domeinen (Domains) |
| WordPress | Websites |
| Mollie, SnelStart | Facturatie (Invoicing) |
| Google Ads, Google Tag Manager | Google Workspace |

Ticking one of those **pulls in what it needs**. Unticking a module leaves the integrations that
depended on it ticked-but-not-saved with an amber line naming what is missing, so you find out
before you save rather than through a refusal.

Because the two screens edit one list, each names its casualties **on the other screen, by
name**: switching Domeinen off under Modules tells you Cloudflare and OXXA go with it, before you
save, on a screen that does not even list them.

<Aside type="note" title="Deliberately not a list of nice-to-haves">
Google Workspace requires nothing, though it enriches interactions, tasks and leave. Uptime Kuma
requires nothing either, because its panels attach to Websites **or** Domains, and a list of
requirements joined by "and" cannot say that. Over-declaring would make you switch on a module
you did not want.
</Aside>

## Licensing and modules

Klanten (Clients), Contactpersonen (Contacts), Taken (Tasks) and Meldingen (Notifications)
are part of the freely usable core. Every other module and every integration is licensed. What a key covers and how you install one is on
[Licenses](/en/docs/admin/licenses/).

Two things meet on this screen:

- **Enabling a licensed module for the first time** only works while a valid key or the trial
  period covers it. Otherwise the checkbox is already blocked, and saving reports "This module
  requires a license."
- **A module that is already on stays on when its license expires.** It becomes read-only:
  reading, searching and exporting keep working, changing does not. The checkbox stays usable on
  purpose, so you can still switch the module off if you have stopped using it.

Elsewhere in the app the same thing shows up as a locked button. Clicking it opens the
"Unlock …" dialog. If you are the instance owner it offers **Naar licentie-instellingen** (Go to
license settings); if you are not, it tells you who to ask instead.

<Aside type="tip">
AI assistance and the MCP server are not on this screen. They are platform capabilities rather
than modules and have their own screens: *Instellingen → AI* and *Instellingen →
Service-accounts* (Service accounts). They do appear on the license page like everything else.
</Aside>

## During installation

The first-run wizard (*Installatie instellen*, Set up this installation) offers the same choice
under the heading **Modules**. What you tick there is only the starting point; you change it
afterwards on this screen with no consequences.

## Permissions

| Permission | What it grants | Held by default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `settings.branding.write` | Change branding and enabled modules | Owner, Administrator |

It is one permission for both screens: whoever may switch modules on may also change the
branding. Installing a license key is not part of it — only the instance owner can do that.

## Good to know

- Switching a module off leaves your roles untouched. The permissions stay as they were and
  apply again the moment the module comes back on.
- A module that arrives with an update shows up in this list by itself and stays off until
  somebody ticks it here. Upgrading alone therefore never changes your workspace.
- If you want a module but not in the sidebar, or under a different name, *Instellingen →
  Navigatie* (Settings → Navigation) is the right place. Switching it off is a heavier
  instrument than you need.
