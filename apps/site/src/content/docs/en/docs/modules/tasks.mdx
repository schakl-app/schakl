Tasks are the work itself: what has to happen, for which client, by whom and when. In schakl.
those four questions are not optional fields: a task without a client is on no client record, a
task without a deadline on no screen that is about time, and a task without anyone on nobody's
board. So every way of making a task asks for them straight away.

![The task board, grouped by when things are due.](/img/docs/en-tasks.png)

## Where to find it

**Taken** (Tasks) in the main menu. Every client record also carries a **Taken** panel, and
every project page a to-do list. The settings screens are:

- *Instellingen → Taakstatussen* (Settings → Task statuses): the statuses of your tasks
- *Instellingen → Taaklabels* (Settings → Task labels): coloured labels
- *Instellingen → Taken* (Settings → Tasks): the task address that turns an e-mail into a task
- *Taken → Sjablonen* (Tasks → Templates): task templates and checklist templates on one screen

Tasks is part of schakl.'s open core, so it needs no licence key. The AI features on this page
(dictation, changing a task in words, filling in a mailed task) need your own AI provider; see
[AI assistance](/en/docs/modules/ai/).

## The board

Opening **Taken** shows **your** tasks first, not everyone's. To see the rest, pick another
assignee or **Geen** (None) in the person picker.

The board opens on the question of when something is due, not on the order somebody once
dragged cards into. The sections are **Over tijd** (overdue), **Vandaag** (today), **Deze week**
(this week — up to and including the coming Sunday, not "the next seven days") and **Later**;
finished tasks sit folded under **Afgerond**. Inside a section the earliest deadline comes
first, and on the same date the highest priority. Only **Over tijd** and **Vandaag** stand out in
colour; a high-priority task gets a rail down its left edge. Every deadline also states the
distance: "20 Aug · 4 days late". To look per status instead, set **Groeperen** (group by) to
**Status**; that lives in the URL, so the view can be shared.

A repeating task on a schedule shows as one row on the board: the next open task with a
"↻ +11" mark. Click it and the series unfolds underneath; **Hele reeks bekijken** (view the whole
series) lists every task in it. A task from the series that is due today or already late always
stays a row of its own, so the overdue count is right.

You can filter on client, project, assignee, label and deadline, plus free text. **Zonder
vervaldatum** (no deadline) gathers the tasks that were created before a deadline became
compulsory, so you can give them one — one at a time, or a whole selection at once through the
✎. You choose your own columns and that choice is remembered.

The ✎ switches the checkboxes on and lets you change a whole selection at once: **status**,
**assignee**, **priority**, **project**, **client** and **deadline**. A deadline, a client and
an assignee can be set that way but never emptied. Rows that are refused (a task whose deadline
needs a reason, say) are reported per row; the rest still go through.

Finish a task and you are taken back to the board, on the same slice you came from: the work on
that card is done.

## Creating a task

There are four doors, and they all ask the same minimum: a **title**, a **client**, a **due
date** and **who is on it**.

- **Nieuwe taak** (New task) on the board, in a client's header or in the client's **Taken**
  panel opens a small dialog. On a client page the client is already filled in, and you
  yourself are a chip among the assignees (removable). The task only exists after **Aanmaken**
  (Create), and you land on its page to fill in the rest. A task you half thought of and
  dismissed therefore never sits on the board as an empty row.
- The **＋** inside a picker creates a task the same way and selects it at once, without leaving
  the page.
- **Inspreken** (dictate) and **e-mail to the task address**: see below.
- Through the API, MCP or the assistant. If a call names nobody, the task goes to the project's
  responsible person, else the client's, else whoever created it.

Naming a project is naming the client, because a project has exactly one. Tasks created before a
client became compulsory stay fully editable; only emptying the client is no longer possible.

### Dictating a task

Beside **Nieuwe taak** sits **Inspreken**. You say the task in one go, the way you would explain
it: "make a task for Jansen: new homepage copy, by Friday at the latest, high priority; first
write a draft, then review with the client, then put it into WordPress; count three hours".
schakl. transcribes it and turns it into a draft with title, description, deadline, priority,
client, project, assignee, labels, time budget, steps and links.

<Steps>
1. Press **Inspreken** and speak (up to five minutes). A second recording is added after the
   first, because you do not have to say everything in one breath.
2. The transcript sits under **Wat je zei** (what you said). If a name is wrong, correct it and
   press **Opnieuw verwerken** (process again).
3. Go through the draft. Whatever schakl. filled in is marked **Door schakl ingevuld**.
4. **Taak aanmaken** (create task) writes everything in one go, with the steps as a checklist.
   Until that click nothing is saved.
</Steps>

A name schakl. cannot recognise with confidence leaves the field empty rather than picking a
different client; **Taak aanmaken** stays disabled until a client and a colleague are chosen.
Dictation needs a speech provider and the AI feature **Taak inspreken, aanpassen en plannen**.

### Mailing a task to the task address

Under *Instellingen → Taken* you set a **Taakadres** (task address), for example
`taak@youragency.com`. Create it as an alias or group in your own mail domain. A colleague mails
that address (a note, a forwarded client mail, a photo) and a task appears on their board.
schakl. reads it through the sender's connected mailbox, via Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, so
no separate mail server is involved. The sender is also who creates the task: someone who may not
create tasks, or may not see the client, is refused here exactly as they would be anywhere else.

- **The subject** becomes the title. You name the client in square brackets:
  "[Nova Fietsen] Renew SSL certificate".
- **Lines at the top of the mail** fill in fields: `klant:`, `voor:`, `deadline:`, `project:`,
  `labels:` and `prioriteit:` (client, for, deadline, project, labels, priority). English words
  work too. A deadline may be a date or a word such as "Friday", and "by 1 October" in running
  text is read as well. If the mail names no deadline, **Standaarddeadline** (default deadline,
  in days after receipt) applies.
- **A forwarded client mail** is recognised by the people in it, when they are contacts of exactly
  one client. That forwarded mail is filed on the task as a contact moment, under the original
  sender's name and date. Your own words above it become the description, without your
  signature. Attachments come along; one that is not allowed is named in the task.
- With the AI feature **E-mail naar taakadres** (mail to task address) switched on, schakl. fills
  in what your own words left open (a client, a colleague, a deadline, steps and links) and never
  overwrites what you wrote. The description is then schakl.'s summary of your words rather than
  the words themselves.
- **One mail may become several tasks.** Empty a phone call into one mail ("the DNS for Nova, the
  newsletter for Klokuus, and Jan still has to check the contract") and schakl. makes a task per
  piece of work that can be assigned, dated and finished on its own, up to eight. Work for
  different clients is always split; the steps of one job stay one task with a checklist; when in
  doubt, one task. What you typed yourself (`klant:`, `voor:`, `deadline:`, `[Client]`) applies to
  every task. The forwarded mail is filed once, on all of them, and each attachment goes with the
  task that names it. Splitting is the model's call, so it needs the AI feature.

If the client cannot be determined, the mail waits under **Taken → E-mailinbox** (e-mail inbox).
That tab only appears while something is waiting; you pick the client and finish the task. A mail
that became several tasks waits whole when any of them lacks a client: the card lists the planned
tasks, the client you pick fills only the ones that had none, **Maak er toch één taak van** (make
it one task instead) folds them into one task with each as a step, and **3 taken aanmaken**
(create 3 tasks) creates them all. Either way you get a notification saying for which client, for whom and with which deadline the task was
made, and which of those schakl. chose itself. A mail sent only to the task address never becomes
a contact moment; a client mail with the task address in Cc becomes both.

## Setting up statuses

Under *Instellingen → Taakstatussen* you rename, recolour and reorder the statuses, or add your
own. Each status carries three flags:

- **Afgerond** (finished): an end state. It decides when a task counts as done, folds away on
  the board, and triggers a recurrence set to "after completion".
- **Standaard** (default): where new tasks start.
- **Contactmoment vereist**: this status is only reachable through a logged contact moment.

<Aside type="caution">
A status can only be deleted once no tasks are left in it, and the last remaining status can
never be deleted. Move the tasks out first.
</Aside>

## A task

The task page has one mode: you edit every field where you read it. Click the title to rename
it, a property (**Status**, **Prioriteit**, **Toegewezen aan**, **Project**, **Klant**,
**Vervaldatum**, **Tijdbudget**) to change it, the description to write. A pick from a list is
saved at once; click anywhere else and the field closes without changing anything. The status
shows as a dot in its colour beside its name. The time budget is shown against the hours logged
on the task, as "x left" or "x over budget".

![A task with a checklist: ticking an item is using the page rather than editing it, so there is no save button.](/img/docs/en-task-detail.png)

- **Labels**: coloured, org-wide, and creatable from inside a task.
- **Checklists**: several per task, each with a description, and items that may carry their own
  explanation. An item or a list is renamed by clicking its text, and its explanation is edited
  where you read it. **Uit sjabloon** (From template) pulls in an existing checklist; **Opslaan
  als sjabloon** (Save as template) puts this one in the shared library. **Stappen schrijven met
  schakl** (write steps with schakl) builds a checklist from the title and the description.
- **Links & bijlagen**: URLs plus real files. You paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V, images show as
  thumbnails and open in a viewer, and per file the eye icon decides whether the client may see
  it. See [Files](/en/docs/modules/files/).
- **Reacties** (comments): with @mentions of colleagues and contacts, `#` references to other
  tasks, and images pasted into the text. You can reply to a comment; a long conversation folds
  its older comments away ("Toon 26 oudere reacties"). **Volgorde** (order) chooses **Nieuwste
  eerst** (newest first) or **Oudste eerst** (oldest first); schakl. remembers it per person, and
  replies always sit under their question. A notification about a comment opens the task on that
  exact comment, with the reply box ready underneath.
- **Activiteit**: everything that happened to the task, from creation to an extended deadline
  and its reason. The name of whoever did it is snapshotted, so it stays readable after the
  account is gone.

Tick the last open item and schakl. offers to move the task to its finished status.

### Changing a task in words

Under the description sits the **Aanpassen met schakl** box (adjust with schakl). You type or
speak what should change: "add a step for the DNS change, deadline Friday, note that the client
wants it in blue". schakl. reads the whole card and applies the change **as you**: every edit is
on the activity trail under your name and passes the same rules as an ordinary edit, so a later
deadline takes your sentence as its reason. The client and project are never changed this way.
The box is only drawn when the AI feature is on and you may edit the task.

<Aside type="caution">
**Every task has a deadline.** Without one it appears on none of the screens that are about
time — not under **Over tijd**, not on the agenda's deadline feed, not in the overdue counts on
your dashboard — so schakl. asks for one wherever a task is created and refuses to let you
empty it afterwards. A deadline is not the same as a planned block: planning the work into the
agenda stays entirely optional.

Moving a deadline **later** demands a reason. In the prompt that appears you either fill in a
reason and press **Bevestigen** (confirm), which saves the new date and the reason together, or
keep the old date. The reason is written into the task's activity trail.
</Aside>

### Two switches that reach beyond the task

- **Alleen afsluiten met een contactmoment** (close only with a contact moment): this one task
  may only reach a finished status through a contact moment designated as its closer.
  Independent of the status flag, so a single task can demand it without the whole status doing
  so.
- **Zichtbaar voor klant** (visible to client): off by default. When on, a portal contact of
  that client can see the task and comment on it. A client never sees a task's labels.

A task can also be assigned to a **contact of its own client** instead of to an employee:
"waiting on the client". The task then becomes visible to the client by itself, appears among
that contact's own tasks in the portal, and if they hold an active portal login they get an
e-mail about it. Never an employee and a contact at once.

## Planning it on the calendar

A deadline is not the same thing as the moment you work on it. Under **Agenda → Nieuw →
Inplannen** (Calendar → New → Schedule), and from the **Planning** panel on the task itself,
you plan blocks:

<Steps>
1. Pick the task; its time budget and deadline are shown beside it.
2. Choose **Voor wie** (for whom): one or more colleagues as chips, by default everyone on the
   task. Yourself always; a colleague only with the matching permission. One block is made per
   person.
3. Fill in **Datum**, **Starttijd**, **Duur (minuten)** and optionally a **Notitie**.
4. Beside the form is that day, one column per person: what is already planned, leave, and their
   Google or Outlook appointments. The new block is drawn dashed on top, and above it you read
   **Geen overlap** (no overlap) or **Overlap: 2**, with what each clash is.
5. The block appears on the Agenda and can be dragged to another day.
6. Once the block has passed it offers **Uren registreren** (log time). That creates the real
   time entry and marks the block as logged, so you never write it twice.
</Steps>

For a colleague you only see **Bezet** (busy) and the time window, unless you may read their
planning. An appointment from someone's own Google calendar is only ever a time window for
anyone, never a title. One task may carry several blocks; with the Google or Microsoft
integration connected they also land in that person's calendar, titled "Client: Task".

## Recurrence

A task can repeat **Dagelijks**, **Wekelijks**, **Maandelijks**, **Per kwartaal** or
**Jaarlijks**, on a fixed day if you want one: a weekday, a day of the month, or a weekday of the
month ("every month on the second Tuesday"). There are two modes:

- **Na afronden** (after completion): the next task appears the moment you finish this one.
- **Op schema** (on schedule): every task for the coming year is created straight away, each on
  its own date, and the rule keeps a year ahead ready. So you can see a quarter ahead of what is
  coming, and hand work over before it arrives.

With **Op schema**, one rule is one series. The rule lives on the first task; every other task in
the series says what it belongs to and links to **Regel bewerken** (edit rule). Change the rhythm
and the future tasks not yet finished are laid out again; anything already done stays. Each next
task carries title, description, priority, client and project, assignees, labels, time budget,
links and checklists (unticked again); comments, attachments and planned blocks do not.

Hand a task in a series to someone else and schakl. asks: **Alleen deze taak** (this task only) or
**Deze en alle volgende** (this one and all following). All following moves the assignees of the
later tasks, and also the blocks already planned and the name in the rule's planning. Useful when
someone leaves.

With **Plan elke volgende taak meteen in** (plan every next task straight away) each new task gets
its time blocks at once. **Blok toevoegen** (add block) puts down more than one, each with its own
moment (**Op de deadline**, **Dagen vóór de deadline**, **Dagen na de deadline**, **Een weekdag
van die week**, **Een weekdag van de maand** or **Een dag van de maand**), its own time and length,
its own people and a note. The preview shows the real dates of the next occurrence; a block that
would fall before today is never booked.

## Templates

**Taken → Sjablonen** holds two libraries. A **takensjabloon** (task template) is a named set of
tasks, each with a priority, **Deadline na** so many days, a time budget, an assignee or
**Verantwoordelijke van de klant** (the client's responsible person, resolved at apply time),
and a checklist. Apply it **Handmatig** (manually) from a client record with **Sjabloon
toepassen**, or automatically **Bij klantstatus** (on client status): the tasks appear as soon
as a client reaches that status. A subscription type can roll its templates out the first time
an agreement becomes active. A template line without **Deadline na** gets the day it is applied
as its deadline. **Checklistsjablonen** (checklist templates) are reusable checklists anyone can
attach to a task.

## Reminders

A daily job announces what is overdue and what falls inside your own threshold; you set that
per person under *Instellingen → Meldingen* (Settings → Notifications), field **Bijna verlopen
(dagen)** (due soon, in days). A task announces itself once per deadline, and again as soon as
someone moves it. Finish the task and the reminder disappears for everyone who had not opened it
yet.

## Permissions

| Permission | Held by default |
| --- | --- |
| `tasks.task.read` | Administrator, Member, Client |
| `tasks.task.create` | Administrator, Member |
| `tasks.task.write` | Administrator (any), Member (own) |
| `tasks.task.delete` | Administrator |
| `tasks.comment.write` | Administrator (any), Member and Client (own) |
| `tasks.schedule.read` | Administrator (anyone's), Member and Client (own) |
| `tasks.schedule.write` | Administrator (anyone's), Member (their own) |
| `tasks.label.write` · `tasks.status.write` | Administrator |
| `tasks.template.write` · `tasks.template.apply` · `tasks.checklist_template.write` | Administrator |
| `tasks.settings.manage` | Administrator: *Instellingen → Taken* and the task address |

For a task, "own" means **the task is assigned to you**. A member therefore edits their own
tasks and not somebody else's. For a client, "own" on the planning means blocks on a task they
may see, without the internal note. All of it is adjustable per role under *Instellingen → Rollen*
(Settings → Roles).

## Good to know

- **Deleting a task takes its comments, checklists, links and planned blocks with it.** Hours
  already logged survive; they simply lose their task link. Delete the first task of a series
  and the future tasks not yet finished go with it.
- **Import always creates new tasks.** Task titles legitimately repeat, so there is no honest
  key to update on. Client and due date are required columns, and the preview names the row
  where they are missing. The `assignee` column matches on the employee's e-mail address, and
  `status` is checked against your own status list. Exporting works normally; see
  [Import & export](/en/docs/modules/import-export/).
- **A task from an automation rule** takes the client of the task or client the rule fired on,
  and a deadline from the rule itself (by default the day it fires).
- **The Sjablonen tab only appears for people allowed to manage templates**; a portal client
  never reaches it.
- The **Dashboard** carries the widgets **Mijn openstaande taken** (my open tasks) and
  **Openstaande taken per project / klant**, and the **Agenda** the feeds **Ingeplande taken**
  (scheduled tasks) and **Taakdeadlines** (red once something is overdue).
