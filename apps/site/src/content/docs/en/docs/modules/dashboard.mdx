The first screen after signing in answers one question: *what am I doing today?* It is a board of
tiles, each contributed by a module you have switched on, each showing only what the person
looking at it is allowed to see.

![My day: tiles you choose and order yourself, with today's work on the left and where things stand on the right.](/img/docs/en-dashboard.png)

## Where to find it

**Dashboard** in the main menu, and the page you land on after logging in. Your own layout is
edited in place with **Aanpassen** (Customise); the layout everyone starts from is set under
*Instellingen → Dashboard* (Settings → Dashboard).

## What is on it

Every tile comes from a module. Switch a module off and its tiles disappear from the gallery
along with it; hold no permission for what a tile reads, and it is never offered to you in the
first place — a dashboard does not draw a card that would refuse.

| Tile | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Uren vandaag | What you have logged today, and whether a timer is running |
| Team deze maand | Total, billable share, revenue, and what still needs approving |
| Mijn openstaande taken | Yours, in four sections: overdue, today, this week and later |
| Openstaande taken per project / klant | Where the open work sits: per row how many are late, due today and due this week, ranked by urgency |
| Verlof | Your own balance, in hours and in days |
| Vandaag afwezig | Who is away, today |
| Openstaande facturen | What is outstanding, and how much of it is overdue |
| Openstaande offertes | Quotes still waiting for an answer |
| Budgetstatus | Every hour budget as over, almost spent or within, with the hours over |
| Projectbudgetten | The budgeted projects burning hottest, with their hours bar |
| Terugkerende omzet | The retainer book, per year, and what renews next |
| Nog te factureren | Work that is finished and not yet on an invoice |
| E-mails te beoordelen | Mail waiting for you to accept or reject it onto a client |
| Marketing | The client figures, in summary |
| Rapportages | The latest reports, and their state |

<Aside type="note" title="A tile shows your work, not the agency's">
"Mijn openstaande taken" is yours. "Team deze maand" is the agency's, and it is only offered to
somebody holding the permission to read other people's hours. That split is why the same
dashboard is useful to a developer and to an owner without either of them configuring anything.
</Aside>

## Every figure opens the list it counts

A tile counts over the whole set, not over the rows it happens to show, and every figure opens
exactly the list it is about.

- **Mijn openstaande taken** has four sections: *Over tijd* (overdue), *Vandaag* (today), *Deze
  week* (this week) and *Later*. Each heading carries its count and opens the task list with the
  same filter, so the heading and the list always agree. *Vandaag* is drawn at zero too, with its
  own sentence, so "nothing today" never looks like a missing section. Every row names the date and
  the distance (*24 aug · over 3 dagen*, *6 dagen te laat*); *Later* starts folded. Tasks due on the
  same day are ordered by priority.
- **Openstaande taken per project / klant** puts on each row how many tasks are late, due today and
  due this week, and ranks on that; a zero draws nothing. Every number opens those tasks.
- **Budgetstatus** divides every hour budget into three bands: *Over budget*, *Bijna op* (almost
  spent) and *Binnen budget* (within budget), with the hours over summed under the first. Each band
  opens the project list with the same filter; an over-budget project visibly runs past the budget
  line in its bar.

The colours are the state's (late, today, soon, fine) and never your brand colour, and every state
carries a glyph as well, so the tile still reads in grey. When a tile is capped, it says so and
points to the rest.

## Making it yours

<Steps>
1. Press **Aanpassen** (Customise). The board switches to edit mode.
2. Drag tiles into the order you want. Some are half-width and some full-width; the grid keeps
   its own shape.
3. Add one from the gallery, or take one off the board. Removing a tile never removes anything
   but the tile.
4. Leave edit mode. Your layout is saved against your account, not against the browser, so it
   follows you to another device.
</Steps>

An administrator sets the **house layout** under *Instellingen → Dashboard*. That is what
everybody who has not arranged their own board sees — including everybody on their first day.
Once somebody has customised their own, the house layout stops applying to them; there is
deliberately no button that overwrites a colleague's arrangement.

## The client's dashboard is a different board

A client with a portal login lands on a board of their own: one column, their tasks first, then
their report, then the figures. It draws from the same machinery and a different gallery: a tile
that links into a screen a client cannot open is never offered there, and your team's house layout
does not apply to them. Their tiles are *Jullie taken*, *Werkzaamheden*, *Jullie rapportage*,
*Marketing* (the curated version, not the agency's full set), *Mijn abonnementen*, *Mijn facturen*
and *Mijn domeinnamen*. With several companies the board shows one company at a time and every tile
switches along. See [Client portal](/en/docs/modules/portal/).

## Permissions

The dashboard itself declares none — it is your own view of your own workspace. Each tile carries
the permission of the data behind it, so a board is exactly as informative as its viewer is
allowed to be. What is gated is arranging a board and, separately, deciding the one everybody
starts from:

| Permission | What it opens | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dashboard.prefs.read` | Read a layout | Everyone |
| `dashboard.prefs.write` | Arrange **your own** board | Everyone |
| `settings.dashboard.manage` | Set the house layout everyone starts from | Administrator |
