Leave is about your own employees, not your clients. How many hours a request costs is worked out
by the server from that employee's work schedule on that particular day, never from a rule of
thumb like "a day is eight hours".

![Leave: your own balance, your requests, and the team roster.](/img/docs/en-leave.png)

## Where to find it

**Verlof** (Leave) sits in the main menu (`/leave`). Anyone who may approve sees two tabs: *Mijn
verlof* (My leave) and *Team*. Organisation-wide configuration lives under *Instellingen → Verlof*
(Settings → Leave); one employee's contract, working week and free time under *Instellingen →
Team & gebruikers → ⋯ → Dienstverband* (Settings → Team & users → ⋯ → Employment), and in the ⋯
menu of the team roster.

Leave is a licensed module, switched on under *Instellingen → Modules* (Settings → Modules).

## Filing a request

*Mijn verlof* shows your balances, your own requests and the **Verlof aanvragen** (Request leave)
button. You pick the **Type**, the **Eerste dag** (First day) and the **Laatste dag** (Last day).
**Deel van een dag** (Part of a day) reveals **Van** and **Tot** (From / To); leave one empty and
it means the start or the end of the working day.

While you type, the server calculates along: the number of **Uren** (Hours), the equivalent in
working days, and a per-day breakdown naming why a day costs nothing (*feestdag* public holiday,
*geen werkdag* not a working day, *buiten werktijd* outside working hours). Ask for more than your
balance holds and the form warns but still submits; the manager decides and sees the shortfall
again on the approval queue. An approver can depart from the schedule with **Uren handmatig
instellen** (Set hours manually), which is recorded against their name.

## Approving

*Verlof → Team* opens with **Goed te keuren** (To approve). Each request shows its hours, what
would be left of the yearly balance, and whether the balance would be exceeded. **Goedkeuren**
(Approve) and **Afwijzen** (Reject) work per row or over a selection; rejecting lets you add a
reason. A request edited after approval returns labelled *Bewerkt, opnieuw ter goedkeuring*
(Edited, awaiting approval again). **Verlof registreren** (Register leave) records leave in a
colleague's name, a sick day for instance. Below that sit the whole team's balances and requests
per year.

![The Team tab: what needs approving, and below it the balance per employee. A freelancer deliberately accrues no pot.](/img/docs/en-leave-team.png)

## Balances, groups and expiry

Leave types sharing a **balansgroep** (balance group) present the employee with one figure. Out of
the box those are *Wettelijke vakantie* (statutory vacation: 4 weeks a year, carry-over expiring
after 6 months) and *Bovenwettelijke vakantie* (extra-statutory: 1 week, 60 months), shown
together as **Vakantieverlof**. The pots stay separate, so the distinction and the differing
expiry dates survive. Consumption favours the employee: a request draws from the pot that expires
soonest first. The balance card says what is expiring soon and what has lapsed.

Yearly entitlements are derived from the contract and recalculated the moment that contract
changes. An entitlement set by hand under *Instellingen → Verlof → Contract & saldi* (Contract &
entitlements) is marked **Handmatig** (Manual) and is never overwritten by that recalculation.

## Working week, contract and free time

The **Dienstverband** wizard has three steps: *Contract* (start, end or open-ended, contract hours
per week), *Werkweek* (Working week: per weekday a working window with any number of break
windows, plus a button to copy the day onto the other working days) and *Vrije tijd* (Free time).
Hours per week is derived from the schedule, never typed. A changed contract is a new row rather
than an edit, and **Beëindigen** (Terminate) only records the end date, which is what keeps last
year's leave priced at last year's roster.

The third step asks the one question nothing can infer: how does this employee get the hours below
the full-time norm? *As free time to schedule themselves*, *Built into the roster* (nothing
accrues), or *An agreed number of hours*. With the first choice you place a pattern right away: a
**number of days per year** (spread on the same weekday, sliding past public holidays so the count
still lands) or a **fixed rhythm** ("every two weeks on Friday"). Those days go onto the calendar
as pre-approved leave, and the **Vrije tijd** card on *Mijn verlof* offers **Verplaatsen** (Move)
or **Annuleren** (Cancel) per day.

<Aside type="caution">
Lower a contract and the balance can end up smaller than what is already on the calendar. Those
days are never dropped silently: the wizard lists them and offers **Dagen terugnemen** (Withdraw
days).
</Aside>

## Setting it up

<Steps>

1. **Verloftypen** (Leave types): a name per language, colour, paid, counts towards a balance,
   requires approval, weeks per year, expiry, balance group, and how the calendar draws it
   (*Hele dag* all day, or *Per uur* by the hour). Six are seeded, among them *Ziek* (Sick: no
   approval, no balance).

2. **Standaard werkrooster** (Default work schedule): the week everyone without their own follows.
   Out of the box 08:30–17:00 with a 12:30–13:00 break, so 8 hours a day.

3. **Goedkeuringsbeleid** (Approval policy): self-approval is off, so an approver's own request
   goes to the other approvers. An organisation with a single approver may always manage its own.

4. **Terugkerende vrije tijd** (Recurring free time): how far ahead free days are placed for an
   open-ended contract. A contract with an end date is filled up to that date.

5. **Uurtarief** (Hourly rate): the default rate for everyone without one of their own.

6. **Feestdagen** (Public holidays): press the import button (it names the year), then switch off
   the days you actually work. Every December a background job tops up the following year.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
Switch **off** a holiday you work (Good Friday, for example) rather than deleting it. Switching it
off keeps it out of every calculation and survives the next import; a deleted one simply comes
back.
</Aside>

## Elsewhere in the platform

Approved leave appears as its own **Verlof** row on the weekly timesheet under Uren (Time) and
never counts as worked time. In the calendar, *Verlof (team)* and *Feestdagen* are two separate
feeds; dragging a day moves the request, after which the server recalculates the hours and asks
for approval again if it has to. Three dashboard widgets exist: your own balance, the queue
waiting on you, and who is away today. With the Google Workspace integration on, approved leave is
mirrored into the employee's own Google Calendar.

## Permissions

| Permission | Default |
| --- | --- |
| `leave.request.read` | Member: own · Administrator: anyone's |
| `leave.request.write` | Member: own · Administrator: anyone's |
| `leave.request.approve` | Administrator |
| `leave.entitlement.read` | Member: own · Administrator: anyone's |
| `leave.entitlement.write` | Administrator |
| `leave.type.write` | Administrator (opens *Instellingen → Verlof*) |
| `leave.profile.manage` | Administrator (schedules and contracts) |
| `leave.holiday.write` | Administrator |
| `leave.rate.read` | Member: own · Administrator: anyone's |
| `leave.rate.write` | Administrator |

The hourly rate is deliberately kept off `leave.profile.manage`: someone may manage schedules
without seeing pay. Roles are configured under *Instellingen → Rollen* (Settings → Roles).

## Good to know

- Leave in the past can only be recorded or changed by someone who may write other people's leave
  (an admin). With self-approval off, their own past is locked too.
- An approved request is never retroactively recalculated. A holiday you add later therefore does
  not rewrite last year's balance.
- Overlapping requests, and spans containing no scheduled hours at all, are refused rather than
  stored.
- Only the Dutch public-holiday calendar is generated. For another country you add the days by
  hand.
- Leave has no import or export: records carrying an approval are meant to be requested.
- The *Goed te keuren* queue does not page; that list is meant to be emptied.
