The clients module is the hub of schakl.: nearly everything in the platform attaches to a
client. Clients live in the main menu; each client has one file page on which the other
modules contribute their panel.

![The client list opens on the working book of business: every status but archived, with the filters as pills above it.](/img/docs/en-companies.png)

## The client file

Besides the base details, the client page shows a panel per enabled module: contacts,
projects, hours, domains, websites, hosting, subscriptions and files. A module you disable
disappears from here automatically — the file is composed of what your organization
actually uses.

The file opens on the work: the vital signs under the header, then the running tasks, hours
and interactions, and only after those the panels you occasionally look up. A panel is drawn
at the width it declares, so every client has the same layout, even when a neighbouring panel
happens to be empty for this one. Panels with nothing in them yet become one strip of
**＋ chips** at the bottom instead of separate empty cards.

- **Every figure opens the list it counts.** The open-task count opens exactly this client's
  tasks, grouped by urgency; the hours open the hours report filtered to this client.
- **A panel shows a page and says what it is not showing**, for example *5 van 23 getoond*,
  with *Nog 18 tonen* or a link to the whole list, already filtered to the client.
- **A detour brings you back.** Open a contact, domain, project or task from a panel, and
  saving, cancelling and deleting all return you to the client. **Uren boeken** opens a dialog
  with the client already filled in; the client page stays underneath.

![The client page is the hub: its vital signs sit under the header, and below them the panels every switched-on module contributes itself.](/img/docs/en-company-detail.png)

At the bottom of the file sits the **activity trail**: who changed what, when, with the
change itself. It is recorded automatically and survives even when an employee later
leaves.

## The client list

The list opens on the working book of business: every status but archived, shown as the
**Niet gearchiveerd** (not archived) pill. **Alles** (all) brings the archive in; both live in
the URL, so you can share them and the back button lands where you were. The export gets
exactly the filter you see on screen. **Mijn klanten** (my clients) shows only the clients
you are on.

The filters sit in the same filter bar every list in schakl. has: search, the status pills in the
status's own colour, and *wissen* (clear) that empties everything at once. On a
phone the bar folds behind one button, which only carries a count when you switched something on;
the default *Niet gearchiveerd* does not count as a filter there.

## Archive or delete

A client you are done with, you **archive**: everything is kept, the client leaves the
working lists and stays reachable under *Alles*.

**Deleting** is for a mistake and moves the client to the **trash** (*Prullenbak*): the client
and everything attached to it hide everywhere, can be restored for thirty days, and are then
gone for good. A client with a history cannot be deleted. If the client has issued invoices
or quotes, domains, hosting accounts, subscriptions, projects or time entries, the dialog
names them with their counts, offers **Archiveren** as the primary button and keeps
**Naar de prullenbak** disabled. Tasks, contact links and documents go along; interactions are
unlinked and kept.

After a delete a notice lets you undo it. While anything is in the trash, the client list
shows **Prullenbak (n)**; under *Instellingen → Prullenbak* (Settings → Trash) you restore or
purge. A client in the trash keeps its client number.

## Contacts

A client has one **primary** contact (marked with the brand colour) and as many additional
contacts as you like. Linking, unlinking and switching the primary happen in the file's
edit mode; quickly adding a new contact works right from the client page.

## Custom fields

Missing a field — a segment, a region code? Under *Instellingen → Aangepaste velden*
(Settings → Custom fields) you define your own typed fields per entity type (text, number, date, select, …),
required if you wish. They automatically appear in the form, on the file and as columns in
the list view.

## Create from wherever you are

Every picker that references a client — on a project, a time entry, a domain — offers
**"＋ add"**: the full create dialog opens in place, and after saving the new client is
selected immediately. You never leave your work because a client didn't exist yet.

## Permissions

Who may view, create, edit or delete clients is set per role under *Instellingen → Rollen* (Settings → Roles) —
like every module, permissions are fine-grained and configured per organization.
