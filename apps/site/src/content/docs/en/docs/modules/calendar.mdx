Planned work, deadlines, who is away and what is in your Google or Outlook calendar are four different
questions that a week only answers together. The agenda puts every one of them on the same grid,
each as a feed you can hide, recolour or split per person.

![The agenda in week view: planned task blocks alongside the all-day band that carries leave and holidays.](/img/docs/en-calendar.png)

## Where to find it

**Agenda** in the main menu. Four views — **Dag**, **Week**, **Maand** and **Jaar** (day, week,
month, year) — with **Vandaag** (Today) and the arrows to move, and a filter button beside them
holding every feed.

The view you leave it on is remembered, so the agenda opens the way you last used it.

## The feeds

| Feed | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| Ingeplande taken | Task blocks, positioned at the hour they were planned for |
| Deadlines | A task's due date, as an all-day marker |
| Teamverlof | Approved and pending absence, per colleague |
| Beschikbaarheid | A freelancer's own deviations from their base week |
| Feestdagen | The public holidays your organisation actually observes |
| Google Agenda | Your own connected Google calendar, plus the shared calendars you tick |
| Outlook-agenda | The same for Microsoft 365: your default calendar plus the calendars you tick |

Each feed can be **switched off**, **recoloured**, and — for planned tasks, leave and
availability — **split per colleague** (**Per collega**), so one colleague's blocks or absence
keep their own colour while the rest share theirs. A colleague's own colour beats the whole
feed's. Those choices are yours and are stored against your account. A colleague who has left no
longer appears in those lists.

Which Google or Outlook calendars come along you choose on your own account page under
**Agenda's** (calendars): your primary or default calendar always syncs, and every shared calendar
you tick gets its own row in the feed menu with its own colour and its own toggle. Untick one and
its events disappear at once. Opening the feed menu costs no request to Google or Microsoft: the
agenda reads from the copy the integration keeps.

<Aside type="note" title="A pending request is drawn faintly, with a question mark">
Leave that has been requested and not yet decided is on the agenda already, at reduced opacity and
prefixed with `?`. That is deliberate: an absence you have not approved yet is still something to
plan around, and hiding it until somebody clicks Approve is how two people end up booked for the
same Thursday.
</Aside>

## Planning work onto it

A task can carry a **planned block**: a day, a start time, a duration and who it is for. That
block appears here, in that person's colour, and it can be dragged to another day or time — the
task moves with it. One task can be planned for several colleagues in one go: each gets a block
of their own. The planning dialog draws that day beside the form, with what each person already
has (planned blocks, leave, appointments), so you see a double booking before you make it. For a
colleague you only see **Bezet** (busy) there, unless you may read their planning.

<Steps>
1. Open a task and use **Inplannen** (Plan) to give it a block.
2. Or press **＋ Nieuw** on the agenda and pick what you are adding.
3. Drag a block to move it. Blocks are draggable; the chips that stand for leave are not, because
   a chip is one occurrence of a rule and moving it would move the rule.
</Steps>

Planning is not the same as logging. A block says *when you intend to do it*; hours are recorded
separately, and finishing a task offers to log the time against the block that was planned for it,
so the same work is never counted twice.

## Google and Outlook, and which way it flows

With the Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 integration connected per employee, your own calendar
appears as a feed here, and what schakl. schedules is mirrored the other way: approved leave and
planned task blocks are written into your calendar. A task block is titled "Client: Task" there,
so your own calendar says whose work it is. Connect both and a block lands in both.

<Aside type="caution" title="The calendar writes one way">
Leave and planned tasks go **out** to Google and Outlook. Events there are read and shown, never
turned into schakl. records. That keeps one authority per kind of thing rather than two systems
each believing they own the same appointment.
</Aside>

A leave type decides how its absence is drawn: an all-day chip, or a positioned block between the
hours the person was scheduled to work. That is a property of the *kind* of leave rather than of
one request — whether an absence reads as "away today" or "away between 08:30 and 17:00" is the
same answer every time — and the mirror in Google or Outlook follows the same flag, so one absence is never an
hour block here and an all-day banner there.

## Permissions

The agenda declares none of its own: each feed carries the permission of the module behind it. So
somebody who may not read other people's hours simply gets an agenda without the team feed, rather
than an agenda that refuses.

| Feed | Behind it |
| --- | --- |
| Ingeplande taken, Deadlines | `tasks.task.read`, and `tasks.schedule.write` to plan |
| Teamverlof | `leave.request.read` |
| Beschikbaarheid | `leave.availability.read` |
| Google Agenda | Your own Google connection |
| Outlook-agenda | Your own Microsoft 365 connection |
