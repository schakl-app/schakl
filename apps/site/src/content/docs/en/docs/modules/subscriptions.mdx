A subscription is a recurring agreement with a client: hosting, maintenance, an SEO retainer. It
records what it costs, how often it invoices and how many hours it includes, and it answers the
question of what is running every month. Something sold once, such as a website build, is an
agreement too, with the interval **Eenmalig** (one-off).

![Retainers with their period, amount and next invoice date.](/img/docs/en-subscriptions.png)

## Where to find it

**Abonnementen** (Subscriptions) in the main menu. Tabs above the section lead to the list itself,
to **Standaardabonnementen** (Standard subscriptions) and to **Abonnementstypes** (Subscription
types). You only see those two with the matching manage permissions; *Instellingen →
Abonnementen* (Settings → Subscriptions) leads to the same screen. The client page, the project
page and the website page each carry an **Abonnementen** panel.

The list is sectioned per standard subscription, with the client as the first column; clicking the
client opens the subscription, and the client page sits in the ⋯ menu. A **Volgende factuur** (next
invoice) date that has passed is drawn as an overdue deadline, and the **Factuurdatum verstreken**
(invoice date passed) tile opens *Nog te factureren* narrowed to subscriptions.

At the top of the list sit **Maandelijks terugkerende inkomsten** (MRR), **Jaarlijks terugkerende
inkomsten** (ARR) and the number of **Actieve abonnementen**. Below them you filter by client, by
status and by your own types.

## Creating a subscription

The quickest way is from the client page: the ＋ on the **Abonnementen** panel opens the same form
in a dialog, with the client already filled in and only this client's projects offered. After
saving you are still on the client page. From the list it goes like this:

<Steps>
1. Click **Nieuw abonnement** (New subscription), optionally after choosing a **Vanuit
   standaardabonnement** (From standard subscription), which prefills the form.
2. Fill in **Naam** (Name), **Klant** (Client) and **Type**. If the agreement sells something
   from your price list, pick it under **Product**: that fills in the name and the amount, and
   both stay yours to change.
3. Set **Status** (Concept / Actief / Gepauzeerd / Opgezegd, i.e. draft / active / paused /
   cancelled), **Interval** (Maandelijks, Per kwartaal, Jaarlijks or Eenmalig), **Bedrag** (Amount),
   **Inbegrepen uren** (Included hours) and the **Startdatum** (Start date).
4. Under **Automatisch factureren** (Automatic invoicing), choose how far invoicing goes on its
   own, or leave it on *Instelling van de organisatie volgen* (follow the organisation setting).
5. Under **Gekoppelde projecten** (Linked projects), link the projects whose hours draw on the
   bundle.
</Steps>

A new subscription defaults to **Actief**. Its **Volgende factuur** (Next invoice) is then derived
for you: the first period boundary from the start date that is still ahead, so an agreement that
has been running for years does not start with a pile of past periods. Only the edit form shows
that date and lets you change it; a date you enter yourself is always honoured.

If it was already invoiced in your previous system, fill in **Gefactureerd tot** (invoiced until).
Periods ending on or before that date are no longer offered anywhere and never created, not by
the nightly run either.
A subscription that arrives through an import deliberately lands as **Concept**, so a bulk import
never quietly starts billing.

## The price has a history

**Bedrag** is not a field you overwrite: every price change adds a row with an effective date. That
is what keeps last year's invoice from being repriced after the fact, and what makes the billing
cycle charge the price that applied on the invoice date.

For a round of increases, use **Prijsverhoging** (Price increase) above the list, or *Prijs
verhogen* in a row's ⋯ menu. You choose the **Aanpassing** (add a percentage, add a fixed amount,
or set a new amount), an **Ingangsdatum** (effective date) and the **Bereik** (scope): all
subscriptions, one type, one subscription, or one standard subscription. Optionally the standard
subscriptions' own prices are updated too. You get a **Voorbeeld** (Preview) first, listing the
current and the new amount per subscription; nothing is written until **Doorvoeren** (Apply).
Cancelled subscriptions are skipped, including when you target one directly.

<Aside type="caution">
Bulk edit can change status, type, client, **Gefactureerd tot**, **Facturering** (billing
direction) and **Notities op de factuur** (notes on the invoice), but deliberately not the price. A price always goes
through **Prijsverhoging**, because that carries an effective date and respects the history.
</Aside>

## Included hours and projects

Under **Gekoppelde projecten** you link the projects the work for this agreement is logged against.
That has two consequences:

- The project takes its **hours budget** from the agreement instead of from its own field. The
  budget field on the project is then locked and reads "Uren komen van abonnement" (hours come from
  a subscription). Where several agreements cover the same project, they add up.
- Work on such a project defaults to **Niet facturabel** (non-billable), because the retainer
  already pays for it.

A non-monthly agreement is converted to a month: 30 included hours per quarter give the project 10
hours per month, and the budget period becomes monthly. Only **active** subscriptions with included
hours count. On the project page the **Abonnementen** panel shows how much of the bundle has been
used this period and whether you are over it.

## The billing cycle

Once a day schakl. checks which **active** subscriptions have reached their **Volgende factuur**
date. For each one the period is offered up at the price that applied on that date, and the next
invoice date moves on by one period. If that date lies several periods back, all of them are
caught up in the same run, not one a night. What the run is going to bill is already listed on
*Nog te factureren* beforehand. What happens next depends on **Automatisch factureren**:

| Level | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Uit (Off) | Nothing is created; the period stays outstanding and you add it yourself in the invoice editor. |
| Concept klaarzetten (Prepare a draft) | A draft invoice appears; you check it and send it. |
| Klaarzetten en definitief maken (Prepare and issue) | The invoice also gets its number and due date, but is not e-mailed. |
| Klaarzetten, definitief maken en mailen (Prepare, issue and send) | The invoice goes to the client with its PDF, unreviewed. |

The cycle always advances, including on *Uit*: a period nobody invoiced stays outstanding and is
offered in the invoice editor, so it is never lost. Draft, paused and cancelled agreements do not
bill.

## Sold once

A one-off sale (a website build, a logo, a migration) is a subscription with the interval
**Eenmalig**. It has no cycle: it is billed once, on its start date (or today, if that has
passed), and it gets everything an agreement has: a standard subscription to start from, a type
whose task templates roll out on activation, linked projects, notes with variables and your own
fields. The moment an invoice bills it, whether the nightly run drafted it or you picked it in the
invoice editor, it moves to **Afgerond** (completed) by itself. Delete, cancel or fully credit
that invoice and it opens again. A one-off agreement never counts towards MRR or ARR.

A standard subscription and an agreement can name the **Product** from your price list that they
sell. That is a record of what is sold, not a link that keeps prices in step: re-pricing the
product later changes nothing on the agreement. Under *Instellingen → Facturatie*, **Producten en
diensten** has an **In gebruik** (in use) column counting the subscriptions and standard
subscriptions that name each product.

## In advance or in arrears

Which period an invoice covers on its invoice date is called **Facturering** (billing):

- **Vooraf** (in advance): the period after the invoice date. Usual for hosting and licences: on
  16 May you invoice the year that begins then.
- **Achteraf** (in arrears): the period before the invoice date. Usual for a retainer billed once
  the month has been delivered. This is the default.

You set it on the **subscription type**. A **standard subscription** may override the type, and a
single **subscription** may choose differently again for itself ("Volg het standaardabonnement of
type" makes it follow again), for an agreement made differently with this client. The subscription
shows which way it bills, and whether that was set on the subscription itself.

<Aside type="caution">
Flipping it re-reads the periods, including the ones already invoiced: what was billed stays
billed, but the dates it stands for move along, so nothing is offered twice. The screen reports
how many subscriptions it touched.
</Aside>

A subscription's page carries the **Gefactureerde termijnen** (invoiced periods) panel: which
periods are already on an invoice, and on which one.

## Linking a subscription to a website

A hosting agreement keeps a website online, and the website can say which one. Tick **Dekt
websites** (covers websites) on a type (the bundled Hosting type already has it); subscriptions of
that type can then be linked to websites of the same client:

- in the subscription form, under **Websites**, which offers only the chosen client's websites;
- or on the website page, in the **Abonnementen** panel: pick one of that client's running
  agreements, or create a new one with ＋, with the client and website already filled in.

The subscription lists the websites it covers under **Dekt** (covers). Untick the type later and
existing links stay until you remove them yourself.

## Standard subscriptions and types

A **standaardabonnement** (standard subscription) is a preset that prefills the create form, such
as "Hosting Basis, € 25 per month, 1 hour included". You make them on the *Standaardabonnementen*
tab, or you save an existing agreement as one with **Opslaan als standaardabonnement** in the row's
⋯ menu. They also record the **Opzegtermijn (dagen)** (notice period in days). Rename a standard
subscription and the subscriptions still carrying that name are renamed with it, and the screen
tells you how many; give a subscription its own name and it stops following. Changing a standard
subscription's price never changes the price of an existing subscription.

**Abonnementstypes** are your own categories (hosting, maintenance, SEO), with a label per
language, a position and an active/inactive switch. More importantly, a type carries
**taaksjablonen bij activatie** (task templates on activation). The first time a subscription of
that type becomes active, those tasks are created on the client. That happens exactly once per
agreement, so pausing and resuming never spawns a second onboarding.

## Notes with variables

The **Notities** (Notes) field understands variables that are stored as tokens and filled in only
when the note is displayed: `{{company_name}}`, `{{subscription_name}}`, `{{type}}`, `{{amount}}`,
`{{interval}}`, `{{included_hours}}`, `{{start_date}}` and `{{brand_name}}`. While you edit, a
**Voorbeeld** (Preview) sits under the field. They work in a standard subscription too, where they
are resolved on every subscription that uses it.

Notes are internal by default. Tick **Notities op de factuur** (notes on the invoice) on a standard
subscription and they print under the lines of every invoice its subscriptions raise, with the
variables filled in at the moment the invoice is made: an amount reads `€ 25,00`, like the rest of
the invoice, and a later price change never rewrites an invoice already sent. A single
subscription can set it differently for itself (*Op elke factuur afdrukken*, print on every
invoice, or *Alleen intern*, internal only). Headings, lists, tables and quotes from the editor are
laid out properly on paper.

## Permissions

Set per role under *Instellingen → Rollen* (Settings → Roles).

| Permission | What it grants | Held by default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `subscriptions.subscription.read` | View subscriptions | Administrator; Client only its own |
| `subscriptions.subscription.write` | Manage subscriptions | Administrator |
| `subscriptions.subscription.delete` | Delete subscriptions | Administrator |
| `subscriptions.type.manage` | Manage subscription types | Administrator |
| `subscriptions.template.manage` | Manage standard subscriptions | Administrator |

Reading is deliberately not a Member default: what a client pays is commercially sensitive. You can
widen it per role. The Client role sees in the portal only its own company's running subscriptions,
with no drafts and none of your internal notes. The Owner role holds everything.

## Good to know

- **Deleting a subscription takes its price history and invoice lines with it.** If you only want
  the billing to stop, set the status to *Gepauzeerd* or *Opgezegd*; the agreement and its history
  stay.
- **On the client page the Abonnementen panel stays empty** for someone without read permission,
  rather than showing an error.
- **Import and export cover three lists**: subscriptions (upserted on name), subscription types (on
  key) and standard subscriptions (on name). Invoice lines and project or task links are
  deliberately not included. See [Import & export](/en/docs/modules/import-export/).
- Subscriptions is a licensed module: when the licence lapses everything stays readable and
  exportable, but writing stops.
