The domains module is the register of every domain you look after for a client: who the
registrar is, where DNS lives, whether e-mail runs on it, when the renewal falls due and what
that renewal costs. What public DNS says about it, schakl. fetches by itself every day.

## Where to find it

In the main menu under **Domeinen & websites → Domeinen** (Domains & websites → Domains). Two
tabs sit above the list: *Domeinen* and *TLD-tarieven* (TLD rates). That second tab is also the
screen behind *Instellingen → Domeinen* (Settings → Domains), "TLD-tarieven voor jaarlijkse
verlengingsfacturen". Every client page carries a **Domeinen** panel with that client's domains,
their price per year and their next renewal.

## Recording a domain

**Nieuw domein** (New domain) opens a single form:

- **Domeinnaam** (Domain name). What you paste is reduced to the bare domain:
  `https://WWW.Example.NL/pagina` is stored as `example.nl`. Scheme, `www.`, port and path are
  stripped, on import too.
- **Startdatum** (Start date). When the registration began. The yearly renewal invoice hangs
  off it.
- **Afwijkende prijs** (Agreed price). A price for *this* domain that overrides the TLD rate.
  Leave it empty and the extension's rate is shown as a hint under the field.
- **Facturatie** (Invoicing). Collapsed on a new domain, with the chosen answers in its header;
  open when you edit an existing one. It holds "Wel factureren / Niet factureren / Volg het
  register" and **Automatisch factureren** (Automatic invoicing).
- **Klant** (Client), **Status** (Actief, Doorverwijzing, Geparkeerd, Verlopen, Inactief) and,
  only on the Doorverwijzing status, **Doorverwijzen naar** (Redirect to).
- **Registrar** and **DNS-provider**, both from your own provider list
  (*Instellingen → Providers*), plus a **Registratiecontact** (Registry contact): the agency,
  the client, an employee or a contact person.
- **E-mail actief** (E-mail active); switch it on and **E-mailprovider** and **E-mailcontact**
  appear.

Every picker offers "＋ toevoegen": a client, contact or provider that does not exist yet is
created without leaving the form.

## What DNS says for itself

Beside the details, the domain page carries a **DNS** card with **Nameservers**, **MX-records**,
**DNSSEC** and **Laatst gecontroleerd** (Last checked). It is a plain public DNS lookup, so it
needs no registrar account and works for every domain. A nightly job refreshes every domain of
every organisation, and a new domain is looked up once immediately after you save it, so it
never sits on "Nooit" (Never). The **Verversen** (Refresh) button does it again on the spot.

DNSSEC has three answers: Ja, Nee and **Onbekend** (Unknown). Unknown means the lookup could not
answer, not that DNSSEC is off.

## Renewal: the date, the price and the invoice

The start date sets the renewal date: schakl. takes the next anniversary still ahead, so a
domain you registered years ago starts at its next birthday and never bills its history. A
nightly job rolls that date a year forward and hands invoicing a line,
`Domeinverlenging <name> (<period>)`. How far that invoice gets on its own is per domain, under **Automatisch
factureren**, or left on the organisation's setting. Renewals that no invoice covers yet are
picked in the invoice editor under *Nog te factureren domeinverlengingen* (Domain renewals still
to invoice).

The price comes from the **TLD-tarieven** tab: one yearly rate per extension, with history.

<Steps>

1. Open *Domeinen → TLD-tarieven* (or *Instellingen → Domeinen*).
2. **Prijs instellen** (Set price) asks for the **TLD**, the **Prijs per jaar** (Price per year)
   and **Geldig vanaf** (Valid from). Today changes it immediately; a future date schedules it.
3. For an across-the-board rise: **Prijsverhoging** (Price increase), with *Percentage erbij*,
   *Vast bedrag erbij* or *Nieuw bedrag instellen*, a **Bereik** (Scope: all TLDs or one) and an
   **Ingangsdatum** (Effective date).
4. **Voorbeeld** (Preview) shows current → new per TLD and how many domains it touches. Only
   **Doorvoeren** (Apply) writes anything.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
Invoices already drafted keep their amount. Price history is the source: an invoice is priced at
the rate that applied on its invoice date, not at today's rate.
</Aside>

## Bill it, don't bill it, or follow the register

A domain list mixes names you renew with names the client registered themselves and merely asked
you to point somewhere. Only a registrar's register knows the difference, so the **Facturatie**
field has three answers:

| Choice | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Wel factureren | The yearly renewal is charged to the client. |
| Niet factureren | Nothing is charged for it. |
| Volg het register | Bill it when a connected register says the agency holds the registration. |

On "Volg het register" a sentence names what decided, for example "Staat in het register van
OXXA, dus dit domein wordt gefactureerd" or "Er is nog geen register gekoppeld, dus dit domein
wordt gefactureerd".

<Aside type="caution">
Only a register that has actually been read may narrow what you bill; a stored credential is not
enough. So with no register connected, nothing changes about what you were already invoicing.
Once a register *has* been fetched, the domains it does not hold stop drafting renewals by
themselves. The OXXA integration was written from OXXA's official API documentation and has never
been exercised against a live OXXA account.
</Aside>

## List, export and bulk

The list is the shared table: you pick your own columns (Domeinnaam, Klant, Status, Registrar,
DNS-provider, DNSSEC, E-mail actief, Volgende verlenging, Prijs, Gefactureerd, Aangemaakt, plus
your own custom fields) and sort on them. Beside the column picker sit **Export/Import** and the
**✎** that switches the checkboxes on. In bulk you can change Status, Klant, Registrar,
DNS-provider, E-mailprovider and Facturatie; clearing Facturatie means "follow the register". The
domain name is never bulk-editable — it *is* the record.

An import upserts on the domain name, normalised, so an export you edit and put back lands on the
same rows. Nameservers, DNSSEC, MX, the next renewal date and the resolved price are export-only.
The TLD rates are their own importable sheet.

At the bottom of the domain page sits the **activity** trail: who changed what, and when.

## Permissions

| Permission | Held by default |
| --- | --- |
| `domains.domain.read` | Administrator, Member, Client |
| `domains.domain.write` | Administrator |
| `domains.domain.delete` | Administrator |
| `domains.tld_price.read` | Administrator, Member |
| `domains.tld_price.manage` | Administrator |

Editable per role under *Instellingen → Rollen* (Settings → Roles). A colleague or portal login
limited to a subset of clients only ever sees those clients' domains. If you would rather a client
login did not see the Domeinen menu at all, take `domains.domain.read` off the *Klant* role.

## Good to know

- Only the statuses Actief, Doorverwijzing and Geparkeerd renew. Verlopen and Inactief get no
  renewal date and therefore no invoice.
- With no price at all (no TLD rate and no agreed price) nothing is drafted and the renewal stays
  outstanding. The moment you price the TLD, billing resumes from the original due date; a year is
  never silently skipped.
- Deleting a domain takes the website that sits on it with it. Not the other way round: deleting a
  website leaves the domain alone.
- The **Doorverwijzing** status is a label on its own. The redirect itself is set up through the
  Cloudflare integration.
